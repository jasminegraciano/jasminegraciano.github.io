The preparation standard

The finish can only be as good as the surface beneath it.

Interior painting is a sequence. Rushing one stage creates problems in the next, so the work is organized from protection through the final walk-through.

Detailed trim and wall finishes depend on careful masking, caulking, repair, and steady application.
01

Set the room and protect it

Furniture, flooring, fixtures, and nearby surfaces are covered or isolated based on the scope and how the room is used.

02

Repair walls, ceilings, and trim

Holes, cracks, rough areas, open joints, and trim imperfections are addressed with the appropriate patching, sanding, spackle, or caulk.

03

Prime where the surface requires it

New sheetrock, repairs, stains, bare materials, or major color changes may need a dedicated primer before finish coats.

04

Apply the selected finish

Brush, roller, or spray methods are chosen for the surface, room conditions, detail level, and production requirements.

05

Review details and return the space

Edges, coverage, trim, touch-ups, and clean-up are inspected so the room is ready to be put back into use.

Finish selection

Choose sheen by surface and daily use.

Color is only one decision. Light, washability, surface condition, room traffic, and the amount of reflection all influence which finish will look and perform best.

Sample before committing. Paint changes with natural light, lamps, surrounding finishes, and time of day.

Flat and Matte

Low-reflection finishes can soften large wall and ceiling areas. Product choice should account for washability and room use.

Eggshell

A subtle sheen often considered for living spaces where a balance of appearance and routine cleaning matters.

Satin

More reflective and typically easier to clean, making it useful to discuss for active rooms and selected trim applications.

Semi-Gloss and Specialty

Often considered for trim, doors, millwork, or decorative applications. Faux finishes can recreate stone, wood, suede, or marble effects.

Interior painting questions

Useful answers before an estimate.

These are the factors that help turn a vague painting idea into a realistic scope, schedule, and proposal.

How much does it cost to paint a room?

Room size is only one factor. Wall condition, ceiling height, trim and doors, repairs, color change, number of coats, furniture, and access all affect the price. A site review produces a more useful estimate than a single room-rate guess.

What affects the cost of painting a whole house?

The estimate depends on room count, total surface area, occupied or vacant conditions, ceilings, trim, closets, stairwells, repairs, preparation, paint system, and whether the project is completed all at once or in phases.

How should I choose interior colors?

Start with fixed elements such as flooring, countertops, tile, wood, and furnishings. Then evaluate samples in the actual room during morning, afternoon, and evening light before approving the final direction.

What should I do before the painters arrive?

Small personal items, fragile decor, and wall hangings should usually be removed. During the estimate, the crew can clarify what should be moved, what can remain, and how large furniture or specialty items will be protected.

How long will my interior project take?

Timing depends on preparation, drying, repairs, room access, crew size, and scope. The project plan should explain the anticipated sequence and how spaces will be returned to use as work progresses.

From inspection to final walk-through

A sequence designed for outdoor conditions.

Exterior painting combines building condition, access, landscaping protection, weather, and cure time. A clear sequence keeps those pieces from becoming surprises.

01

Inspect and define the scope

Surfaces, previous coatings, access, repairs, color goals, and sensitive property areas are reviewed before pricing.

02

Clean and protect

Exterior cleaning removes dirt and organic buildup as appropriate, while plants, fixtures, roofs, windows, and adjacent areas are protected.

03

Scrape, sand, repair, and prime

Failed coating is removed, rough transitions are addressed, open joints or minor defects are corrected, and bare or repaired areas receive the required primer.

04

Apply finish coats in suitable conditions

Application method, coverage, temperature, surface moisture, wind, shade, and forecast are considered throughout the work.

05

Inspect details and restore the site

Coverage, edges, touch-ups, protection removal, clean-up, and the final property walk-through complete the project.

Durability decisions

What belongs in a serious exterior proposal.

The most useful estimate explains what will be painted, how it will be prepared, what is excluded, and how the property will be protected.

Surface condition

The proposal should distinguish sound areas from peeling, bare, stained, repaired, or moisture-affected surfaces.

Access and protection

Ladders, lifts, rooflines, landscaping, windows, fixtures, vehicles, walkways, and neighboring areas can all affect the work plan.

Product system

Primer and finish selections should reflect the substrate, prior coating, exposure, desired appearance, and manufacturer requirements.

Schedule and weather

Exterior schedules need room for surface drying, cure time, changing forecasts, shade, temperature swings, and rain delays.

Closeout

Touch-ups, clean-up, protection removal, remaining repairs, and final review should be addressed before the project is considered complete.

Older properties may require lead-safe planning. The property age, existing coatings, project scope, and applicable requirements should be reviewed before disturbing painted surfaces.
Exterior painting questions

Clear answers before ladders arrive.

These questions help owners compare proposals based on preparation and project control, not price alone.

How much does exterior house painting cost?

Price depends on surface area, stories and access, substrate, coating condition, repairs, trim complexity, preparation, primer, color change, product system, and protection requirements. A site visit is the most reliable way to define the scope.

How do I know whether the exterior is ready for paint?

Peeling, chalking, exposed wood, open joints, staining, mildew, failed caulk, soft areas, or recurring moisture can signal that cleaning or repair is needed before finish coats. The cause of failure matters as much as the visible symptom.

Can you paint while the home is occupied?

Most exterior projects allow the home to remain occupied. Access, entrances, windows, vehicles, pets, landscaping, noise, and daily work zones should be discussed so the property stays usable and safe.

What happens if weather interrupts the schedule?

Exterior schedules should remain flexible enough to protect quality. Surfaces need suitable temperature and moisture conditions, and work may pause or shift when rain, wind, heat, cold, or drying conditions are unfavorable.

How should exterior colors be selected?

Consider roofing, masonry, windows, neighboring finishes, landscape, architectural style, sunlight, and any association or historic requirements. Large exterior samples provide better information than small color chips.

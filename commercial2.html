Before you hire

What a strong commercial proposal should answer.

Price matters, but a reliable commercial painting decision also depends on scope clarity, relevant experience, schedule control, preparation, safety, insurance, and accountability.

Does the contractor understand your type of property?

Ask about the company background, areas of expertise, years in business, offered services, references, and comparable work. A qualified contractor should explain the project in clear terms and connect their experience directly to your building, surfaces, and operating needs.

Is the written scope specific and easy to compare?

A useful estimate should identify the work areas, preparation, repairs, coating system, application method, protection, clean-up, exclusions, and expected sequence. Clear scope language helps owners compare proposals on real value rather than price alone.

How will the work affect normal business activity?

Commercial painting should account for employees, tenants, customers, deliveries, parking, entrances, noise, ventilation, and restricted areas. Discuss work hours, phasing, daily access, and communication before the project starts.

What preparation will each surface require?

Durable results begin with surface analysis and preparation. Depending on the project, that can include washing, scraping, sanding, patching, caulking, priming, or specialty treatments. The coating system should match the existing substrate and exposure.

Are safety, equipment, and insurance addressed?

Confirm that the crew is properly insured and prepared for the access conditions involved. Commercial work can require lifts, sprayers, fall protection, controlled work zones, and other equipment. The proposal should reflect how the team will protect people and property.

What happens at project completion?

Ask how touch-ups, clean-up, inspections, and the final walk-through are handled. A professional contractor should be ready to review the work area with you, address remaining details, and leave the site ready for normal use.
